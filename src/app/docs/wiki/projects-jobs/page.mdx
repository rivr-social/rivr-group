# Projects & jobs

## What it is

The work graph. A **project** is a coordination container with its own wallet,
team, calendar, discussion, and stock. A **job** is a claimable unit of work
inside (or outside) a project; jobs hold **tasks**.

The core discipline: the worker **claims** work finished, and someone with
authority **attests** it. Points and cash only move at attestation — never on a
bare check-off.

<Callout type="note" title="Where these live">
  There is no <code>/projects</code> directory page. Projects are reached from
  the group's <strong>Jobs</strong> tab and open at
  <code>/projects/[id]</code>. Jobs have their own board at <code>/jobs</code>
  and detail at <code>/jobs/[id]</code>.
</Callout>

## How to claim and finish a job

1. Open **`/jobs`** (or the group's Jobs tab) and pick a job. Each card shows a
   pay badge: **Fixed $X**, **Hourly $X/hr**, **N points**, or **Volunteer
   (Thanks)**.
2. Click **Claim** — on the card or on `/jobs/<id>`.
   Claiming **always requires active membership in the owning group** (group and
   ancestor admins also qualify, and they bypass badge gates). A job may
   additionally be badge-gated, admin-gated, slot-capped, or approval-gated —
   approval-gated claims become pending requests.
3. Do the work. If there is a work timer, start it — your check-off links to the
   running work period automatically.
4. **Check off a task.** This records a *claim*, not a completion: the task lands
   in **awaiting attestation**. You can propose a point value with your claim.
5. When the whole job is finished, **claim the job complete** and rate the work's
   **skillfulness required** and **difficulty** (1–100 sliders).

## How to attest work (QA / lead / admin)

1. Open the job. Attesters see extra controls the worker does not.
2. On a task's point chip, **attest** or **reject** the claim. Attesting awards
   the points — the attester may adjust the value. Rejecting or reopening
   deactivates both the claim and its points.
   *A verifier's own check-off attests in one click.*
3. Use the admin-only **Review** tab for the full picture: per-assignee recorded
   work periods across both timer rails, their claim ratings and proposed
   points, their attested points, and the **discrepancies** — claimed with zero
   tracked time, time over `maxHours`, proposed ≠ attested, claim awaiting
   attestation.
4. Correct a recorded period's duration if needed (admins on any row; a worker
   on their own). A running period can't be edited. Hourly pay re-reads the
   corrected value at the next mark-done.
5. **Mark done.** This completes the job, records a contribution edge per
   assignee, and records the cash owed.
6. **Release payment** — a separate, deliberate step that fires the real Stripe
   transfer. See [Wallet & treasury](/docs/wiki/wallet-treasury#how-the-group-gets-money-to-a-worker-release-payment).

Who can attest: the project's **QA**, its **lead**, or a **group-or-ancestor
admin** (`canAttestWork`). QA defaults to the lead and can be any member of the
group, its parent, a sibling, or a child subgroup — or an **affiliated group
agent**, whose admins then hold the authority. Set both on the project's **Team**
tab.

## How pay works

| Pay kind | What happens at mark-done |
| --- | --- |
| **Fixed cash** | equal split among assignees, deterministic remainder |
| **Hourly cash** | rate × stopped work segments, **clamped to the job's `maxHours`** (overage stays visible on the timesheet but doesn't pay) |
| **Points** | task-less jobs carry a job-level point pool, split by peer allocation (below) |
| **Volunteer (Thanks)** | **no cash moves** — see below |

**Where the cash comes from:** a job with a `projectId` debits **that project's
wallet** — the budget the group approved into it. Underfunded → the payout parks
as `pending_funds`; top up and re-run mark-done. It never silently dips into the
group treasury. Jobs outside any project pay from the group settlement wallet.
Every payout is idempotent per assignee — re-running retries only what's pending.

**Splitting job points (Points tab).** Each assignee sets sliders over the
*other* assignees; the pool splits by the normalized average of everyone's
ratings using largest-remainder, and stays an equal split until anyone rates.

**Volunteer pay.** Marking a volunteer job done gives each volunteer a post-hoc
**Thanks voucher**, valued from the skillfulness × difficulty ratings × hours
worked (the same formula the voucher builder uses). The **group redeems it by
transferring Thanks tokens it actually holds** (oldest first) — none are minted.
If the group's pool is short, the payout parks as `volunteer_pending_thanks`
(all-or-nothing per volunteer); top up and re-run. Marking a volunteer job done
opens a dialog with skillfulness/difficulty sliders and a live Thanks-per-hour
preview; those ratings value every volunteer's voucher, each still scaled by
their own hours.

## How to run a project

1. Create it from **`/create` → Project**, or from the group's Jobs tab.
2. Set name, description, budget, deliverables, and a timeframe (start/end in one
   date-time range popover).
3. Open **`/projects/<id>`**. Its tabs: **About**, **Jobs**, **Calendar**,
   **Discussion**, **Team**, **Treasury**, **Stock**.
4. On **Team**, set the **Lead** (coordinator, default attester) and the **QA**.
5. On **Treasury**, use **Move money** to fund the project wallet from the group
   treasury — expenses require a funded wallet. Return unspent balance the same
   way. **Completed projects refuse new funding.**
6. Add jobs from the Jobs tab (Add-job dialog). Active claimants — not just
   admins — can add tasks to a job they've claimed; their proposed points still
   settle through attestation.
7. **Complete the project.** A group admin or the project lead completes it;
   expenses are already first-class debits, so the remaining wallet balance — the
   **net** — sweeps into the owning group's treasury automatically (idempotent).
   Run an explicit net distribution *first* if the net should split beyond the
   owning group. Every subgroup has a treasury to receive it.

## Stock: inventory and needs lists

Orgs, projects, and jobs each get a **Stock** tab with two sub-tabs.

**Inventory** — the tangible resources associated with that object: an org's
owned assets, a project's `projectId`-linked stock, a job's `jobId`-linked stock.
Non-tangible resource kinds (work periods, funds) are filtered out.

**Needs** — no longer a single flat list. Needs live in **named lists**:

1. Open Stock → **Needs**. The legacy **General** list is always there.
2. Click **New list**, name it, and optionally **connect it to a project**.
3. Add a need to a chosen list: name, quantity, note.
4. Edit or remove needs inline; only the object's owner/admins can manage them.
5. **Post as a request** — publish a need as a `Request` post so the community
   can help fill it.
6. Mark a need **fulfilled** → it is **materialized into Inventory**
   server-side. Un-fulfilling leaves the inventory row in place.
7. On an **org** parent you additionally get a read-only **rollup**: every
   subgroup's lists, and under each, the lists of that agent's projects.

## Good to know

- **Job and task ordering is stable** — sorted by `createdAt` then `id`, both
  immutable. Never sort these lists by completion or `updatedAt`; the list used
  to jump on every check-off.
- **Time bounds.** Jobs and tasks carry a work window (start → deadline) and a
  **max-hours budget**. Hourly pay never exceeds the budget.
- **Editing pay type takes effect immediately** — mark-done reads `payKind`
  fresh, so switching a job from fixed to volunteer engages the volunteer branch.
- **Calendar.** Your completed work periods and claimed job windows appear on
  `/calendar` (filterable). Group admins additionally see a per-member "Work
  periods" kind on the group calendar. Projects have their own Calendar tab
  showing their events and jobs.
- **Discussion.** Jobs and projects both have a Discussion tab using the same
  comment thread primitive as events.
- **Subscriptions gate group memberships, never jobs.**
- Guests can view public projects and jobs.
