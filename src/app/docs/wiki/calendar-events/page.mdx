# Calendar, events & virtual meetings

## What it is

Events are the group's scheduled life: gatherings, classes, meetings. An event
can be **in person**, **online** (a link to Zoom or similar), or a **Virtual
Meeting hosted right here** — where the event page itself *is* the venue, backed
by a LiveKit room, and the session can be recorded into a **speaker-identified
transcript** attached to the event.

The calendar is the personal view across all of it, including your own claimed
jobs and completed work periods.

## How to create an event

1. Go to **`/create`** and choose the **Event** tab.
2. Enter the name and description.
3. Set the **date and time** — one range selector sets start and end together (a
   range calendar plus start/end times in a single popover). The same control is
   on the edit page.
4. Choose the **Event Type**:
   - **In person** — add a location.
   - **Online** — add a meeting link or platform.
   - **Virtual Meeting (hosted here)** — no location or link needed; the event
     page becomes the room.
5. Optionally add ticket tiers (tickets are event offerings; add several tiers if
   you need them).
6. Optionally link the event to a project.
7. Create.

## How to attend

1. Open **`/events`** and pick an event, or go straight to **`/events/<id>`**.
2. **RSVP** — going, interested, or none.
3. For paid events, go to **`/events/<id>/tickets`**, choose a tier and quantity,
   and pay by card or Rivr wallet.
4. After buying you land on **`/events/<id>/registered`**, which confirms your
   spot and offers add-to-calendar and share.

## How to run a Virtual Meeting

1. Create the event with type **Virtual Meeting (hosted here)** (above).
2. Open `/events/<id>`. Where an in-person event shows a location, a virtual
   meeting shows **"Virtual Meeting — hosted here"** and the meeting panel.
3. The panel polls status every 30 seconds and shows whether the meeting is
   **scheduled / live**, the participant count, and whether recording is on.
4. Click **Join**. A full-screen LiveKit video conference opens in place.
5. When recording is configured, participants are told the session is recorded
   and that a speaker-labeled transcript will be attached afterwards.
6. Leave with the close control. When the room empties (10-minute timeout) the
   meeting ends.

![A virtual-meeting event page: the header says "Virtual Meeting — hosted here", and the Virtual Meeting panel shows "Opens shortly before the event starts · recorded with transcript" beside the Join meeting button, above Registration and RSVP controls](/wiki/calendar-events/group-02-virtual-meeting-panel.png)

### Who can join

The join lane authorizes against a real electorate — group **manage access**, an
**active membership** (`canPostToGroup`), or an **active RSVP** on the event.
The join **window** is enforced for non-managers: **15 minutes before** the start
until **2 hours after**. Managers can join outside it. Auth resolves through the
remote-viewer-aware session, so an SSO'd federated member is recognized.

### How the transcript gets its speaker labels

Speaker labels are exact **by construction**, not by guesswork:

1. Your LiveKit token's identity **is your authenticated agent id**.
2. When you publish a track, LiveKit's webhook starts a **per-participant audio
   egress** — one recording file per identity.
3. When every egress has ended and the room is finished, each track file is
   transcribed separately, the segments are interleaved on the meeting clock and
   coalesced, and the result is landed as the event's **transcript document** in
   the form `**Name** [MM:SS]: text`.

This is why the transcript never needs diarization guessing — the track owner
already names the speaker.

## How to use the calendar

1. Go to **`/calendar`**.
2. Move by week/month with the arrows; search and filter with the filter popover.
3. Filter by kind, group, and member. Your **claimed job windows** and
   **completed work periods** appear alongside events.
4. Group admins additionally see a per-member **Work periods** kind on the group
   calendar.

## Organizer surfaces

- **`/events/<id>/edit`** — change details; organizer/admin only.
- **`/events/<id>/financials`** — line items, payouts, export; organizer/admin
  only.
- **`/events/<id>/registered`** — the registered view.
- **`/events/<id>/tickets`** — the ticket purchase flow.

## Good to know

- **Tickets price through the same unified fee model** as the marketplace: 3.3% +
  $1.49 + Connect overhead, grossed up over Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢, tax-exclusive
  with Stripe Tax. See [Marketplace & fees](/docs/wiki/marketplace).
- **Event pages are public**; RSVP and ticket purchase require a session; edit
  and financials are organizer-scoped.
- **Virtual meetings need instance configuration.** Without `LIVEKIT_URL` (plus
  API key/secret) the join route returns 503 and the panel explains. Without the
  recording S3 configuration, recording is silently off — the meeting still
  works.
- Room names are **deterministic**: `evt-<eventId>`. Default cap is **100
  participants**; join tokens live 6 hours.
- Google Calendar events are pulled in by the `/api/cron/google-calendar-sync`
  cron where a Google Workspace connector is linked (Settings → Connectors).
- Events can carry a linked transcript document even outside the virtual-meeting
  lane: `POST /api/events/[id]/transcribe` accepts an audio upload, transcribes
  it, and appends the text to the event's transcript document. It requires a
  session and returns 503 when transcription is not configured on the
  deployment.

## Adding a local admission tax (organizer-remitted)

Some cities levy admission taxes that fall on the ORGANIZER — Denver's 10%
Facilities Development Admission Tax is the classic example — and marketplace
rules don't shift them to RIVR. If your event owes one, enable **Add a local
admission tax** on the create-event form: give it the name buyers should see,
your chosen percentage, and — optionally — your tax registration ID for your
own records. It appears as its own separately-stated line at
checkout, is computed on the ticket subtotal, is part of the charged total (RIVR's platform fee and processing gross-up
apply to the full amount charged), and is paid out **to you** with your ticket revenue. **You are
responsible for remitting it to your taxing authority; RIVR does not remit
it.** One organizer tax per event; changes never affect completed orders.

## Venue address & ticket tax

An in-person event needs a structured **venue address** (street, city, state,
ZIP — entered on the create-event form) before paid tickets can be sold:
admissions tax is calculated where the event takes place, not where the buyer
lives. Ticket checkout for paid in-person events refuses until the organizer
adds one. Online events don't need a venue address — their tickets are taxed
at the buyer's address like any digital purchase.
